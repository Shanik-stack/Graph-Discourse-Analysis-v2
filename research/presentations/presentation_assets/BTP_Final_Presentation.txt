End-to-End Pipeline Overview
Indian Institute of Information Technology Kottayam
2
The proposed framework follows a sequential pipeline that transforms raw debate text into structured reasoning insights. Each module is independently evaluable while contributing to the overall system.
Claim Extraction - Identify atomic argumentative claims from raw text.
Stance Detection - Determine support, contradiction, or neutrality between claims.
Graph Construction - Represent claims and relations as directed graphs.
Fallacy Classification - Assign claim-level fallacy labels.
Graph-Based Reasoning - Detect contradictions, cycles, and weak arguments.
Visualization & Analysis - Present interpretable reasoning paths
This modular design enables targeted optimization and benchmarking of each stage.

Claim Extraction Module
Indian Institute of Information Technology Kottayam
3
The first stage of the pipeline focuses on extracting meaningful argumentative units from raw debate text. In the current implementation, sentence-level segmentation is used as a baseline approach to identify candidate claims.
Using the Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK), debate text is segmented into grammatically complete sentences. These sentences are treated as provisional claims and serve as nodes in the stance graph.
Observations
Sentence-level extraction reliably captures explicit claims in structured debates.
However, complex claims spanning multiple sentences or implicit premises are not fully captured.
This limitation motivates future integration of supervised argument-mining models.
Results
Produced clean node sets for downstream stance detection.

Stance Detection Using NLI Models
Indian Institute of Information Technology Kottayam
4
Stance detection determines whether one claim supports, contradicts, or is neutral with respect to another claim. This task is modeled as a Natural Language Inference (NLI) problem.
Each ordered claim pair (cᵢ, cⱼ) is encoded using the RoBERTa-large-MNLI format, where one claim acts as the premise and the other as the hypothesis. The model outputs probabilities for entailment, contradiction, and neutrality.
To ensure reliability, only predictions above a confidence threshold (0.75) are retained as graph edges.
Model Configuration
Model: RoBERTa-large-MNLI
Hidden size: 1024
Output classes: support, contradict, neutral

Stance Graph Construction
Indian Institute of Information Technology Kottayam
6
Extracted claims are represented as vertices in a directed graph. High-confidence stance relations form directed edges annotated with relation type and probability.
Two graphs are constructed:
Stance Graph (Gₛ): Used for structural reasoning.
Logical Graph (Gₗ): Enriched with fallacy labels and metadata.
This representation allows reasoning at both local (edge-level) and global (graph-level) scales.

Efficient Graph Construction at Scale
Indian Institute of Information Technology Kottayam
20
Naive stance detection scales quadratically with the number of claims. To address this, semantic pruning was introduced to eliminate claim pairs that are unlikely to be related.
Using sentence embeddings and similarity thresholds, irrelevant pairs are filtered before NLI evaluation.
Results
5×–10× reduction in runtime on large debates
Minimal loss of meaningful support or contradiction edges
Improved feasibility for real-world discourse analysis
20

Scalability Analysis and Semantic Pruning
Indian Institute of Information Technology Kottayam
21
Initial graph construction required O(n²) stance evaluations, leading to excessive runtime for large debates.
To address this, semantic pruning using Sentence-BERT embeddings was introduced. Claim pairs with cosine similarity below 0.3 were discarded prior to NLI evaluation.
Results
Achieved 5×–10× runtime speedup on EthiX dataset.
Reduced graph density without significantly affecting contradiction detection.
Demonstrates feasibility for large-scale discourse analysis.

Bottleneck in our previous work
Our results in the previous review was bottlenecked by the performance of the stance prediction model.
Although the graph modelling and algorithms were put in place, the overall system did not work for complex graphs/debates.

Thus we have focused on improving the stance prediction model before moving on to further graph analysis algorithms.

Papers we are following
There are 2 main papers we are following to improve our stance model,
GraphNLI: A Graph-based Natural Language Inference Model for Polarity Prediction in Online Debates

- Implements a graph based ML algorithm to perform stance detection, includes argument context by graph traversal. Very relevant to our problem statement of performing stance detection on graphs.

Extractive Fact Decomposition for Interpretable Natural Language Inference in One Forward Pass

- Demonstrates the effectiveness of Claim decomposition from arguments in increasing the accuracy of stance detection

Graph Algorithms for Automatic Analysis
We aim to utilize various Graph Algorithms to analyze the structure of large scale debates.
Some of the algorithms we are using,

1. PageRank: To analyze the support and attack structure of nodes from a global perspective(unlike in_degree which only has a local perspective)

2. Minimum-Cut: - Min-Cut measures the vulnerability of a support graph/sub-graph. It find the minimum amount of claims that have to be disproven or attacked to break the support structure and cohesive argument made in the sub-graph.

3. Graph Clustering: Clustering will help us identify the overall structure of the debate claims overall structure of the debate, from finding the sub-topics discussed in each cluster to the evolution of the overarching ideas in the debate with the advance of time.








In our scenario, the page rank algorithm is done on 3 graphs- exclusive support, exclusive attack and combined support+attack

For the exclusive support+attack graph we find the net Page Rank for each node being: PageRank_{support}(u) - PageRank_{attack}(u)

Page Rank of a support graph gives you the globally most well supported claim/node.

Similarly PageRank of an attack graph gives you the globally most attacked claim/node.
